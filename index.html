Range First, Then Load
PROGRESSION
How to Read Tempo
  • 4-2-1: 4 sec lowering · 2 sec pause in the stretched position · 1 sec lifting.
  • 2-2-1: 2 sec lowering · 2 sec pause at the bottom · 1 sec lifting.
  • The pause is a real pause: no bouncing, no relaxing. If you can't hold the tempo, the weight is too heavy.
Double Progression
01
Reps First
Pick a weight you can hit for the prescribed reps with clean form and the right tempo. Each week, try to add a rep or own the reps better.
02
Then Add Weight
When every set hits the target reps at the same tempo and depth, add the smallest jump next session: 2.5–5 lb per dumbbell, 5–10 lb on barbells and machines.
03
Failure Sets
Dead hangs, German hangs, and wrist rollers go to failure. Log seconds or reps and try to beat last week's number. Stop the set when form or grip quality breaks, not when it hurts.
04
Depth Before Load
VMO squats, Cossacks, ATG split squats, sissy squats, and reverse Nordics earn weight only after you own the full range for every rep.
Range Ladders
Reverse Nordic
Move down when all reps are controlled
  • Box or bench behind you, lean back until you touch it.
  • Lower box, more range.
  • Head to the floor, controlled both ways.
  • Then add load (plate at chest).
Sissy Squat
Same idea: range, then support, then load
  • Hold a rack with both hands, partial range.
  • Full range, still holding.
  • One-hand support, then fingertip touch.
  • Free-standing, then hold a plate.
Jefferson Curl
Slowest progression in the program
  • Bodyweight or a very light plate, standing on a box.
  • Add range below the box before adding weight.
  • Add load in tiny jumps. Never rush this one.
Stop the Movement
  • Sharp or pinching pain in a joint, not just muscle burn or a deep stretch.
  • Knee or back pain that's worse the next morning.
  • Numbness, tingling, or pain that travels.
  • Swap to a comfortable variation and note it in Session Notes.

Your Week
5 lifts + 2 boxing classes
  • Sunday: Leg Day 3 (heaviest hinge day, furthest from boxing)
  • Monday: Upper Body 1
  • Tuesday: Leg Day 1
  • Wednesday: 🥊 Boxing
  • Thursday: Leg Day 2
  • Friday: 🥊 Boxing
  • Saturday: Upper Body 2 (heavy pulling after boxing, not before)
🥊 Boxing Days
  • No two leg days back to back, and no upper day the day before boxing, so your shoulders and grip show up fresh.
  • That's 7 training days. If you need a full rest day, drop the easy recovery work first, then lift Upper 2 after Friday boxing instead of Saturday.
  • Boxing counts as your conditioning. No extra cardio needed.
  • Shoulders or wrists cranky after class? Cut the dead hang, German hang, and wrist roller sets in half that week.
😴
Sleep 7–9 Hours
Consistent bed and wake times. Caffeine cut-off about 8 hours before bed. Dark, cool room, phone away for the last 30 minutes.
🧘
Daily Mobility
Couch stretch and incline pigeon on off days too. 5 minutes is enough. Your knees and hips will thank you on Leg 2.
🚶
Rest Days Still Move
With boxing on Wed and Fri, recovery days are rare. Any extra movement stays easy: a walk or a light sled drag.
💧
Hydrate
Before, during, and after training, especially in the Florida heat. Electrolytes on sweaty days.
🔥
Soreness vs. Pain
Muscle soreness 24–72 hours after tempo and deep-range work is normal. Joint pain that's sharp, spreads, or lingers is not. Back off and adjust.
When to Back Off
01
Watch for These Signs
Under 6 hours of sleep for 2+ nights. Soreness lasting beyond 72 hours. Numbers dropping two sessions in a row. Knees or back feeling cranky on daily stairs.
02
How to Back Off
Drop one set per exercise, take about 10% off the loads, keep the tempo and range. Back to normal once you feel recovered.
03
Consistency Beats Hero Days
Five solid sessions with clean tempo build more than one all-out day and a week of recovering from it.
